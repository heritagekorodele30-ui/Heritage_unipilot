WELCOME & BACKGROUND

About the Department

Summarised from the Nursing Science handbook extracts supplied for this page.

Welcome to the Department of Nursing Science. This handbook guide supports undergraduate students as they navigate the B.N.Sc. Nursing Science curriculum, academic procedures and university regulations.

The generic programme is designed to prepare independent professional nurses who can work effectively within interdisciplinary healthcare teams. It responds to changing disease patterns in Nigeria and across Africa, building competence in clinical care, patient safety and health-facility management.

Programme philosophy: Nursing views people as biopsychosocial beings whose needs guide health-related activities. It combines nursing science with life, social and behavioural sciences to support clients through evidence-based practice across primary, secondary and tertiary care.

LEARNING & DEVELOPMENT

Programme Objectives & Skills

  • Develop nurses who communicate effectively, evaluate values and apply evidence-based healthcare actions.
  • Prepare polyvalent nurses to provide therapeutic care across diverse clinical and community settings.
  • Support individuals, families and communities from varied backgrounds and with different health needs.
  • Promote optimal health while protecting client safety.
  • Connect healthcare delivery to wider social systems and encourage lifelong learning.
  • Build effective teamwork and communication using current educational technologies and research-informed management strategies.
  • Encourage research-led innovation in clinical education, practice and administration.
Distinctive programme features
  • Clinical and community-based learning.
  • Professional development, leadership and management skills.
  • Quality improvement, problem-solving and workplace adaptability.
  • Interdisciplinary teamwork and evidence-informed care.
Professional examinations

The supplied handbook summary indicates UTME entrants sit professional nursing certificate examinations at 400 level. Post-basic midwifery or related professional examinations are indicated at 500 level alongside Direct Entry students. Confirm current examination arrangements with the department and relevant professional regulator.

ENTRY REQUIREMENTS

Admission Requirements

Use this as a guide only; confirm the current year's requirements and cut-off with AAUA Admissions.

UTME · 100 Level

Applicants normally apply through JAMB and must meet the current department-specific UTME cut-off, which the supplied handbook says cannot be below 180. Five SSC/SSCE or equivalent credit passes in no more than two sittings are required, including English Language, Mathematics, Physics, Chemistry and Biology.

Direct Entry · 200 Level

Eligible candidates may include applicants with at least two relevant Advanced Level passes plus required O/L credits; diploma holders with an upper-credit pass and the required O/L qualifications; and Registered Nurse (RN) or Registered Midwife (RM) certificate holders who also meet the stated academic requirements. The handbook summary describes a four-year route.

Pre-degree route

Admission may also be considered through the university's pre-degree programme for candidates who meet O/L requirements and attain the required programme results. Availability and progression rules should be confirmed for the current admission cycle.

Pass mark & degree type

The supplied handbook summary states a 50% pass mark for core courses and describes the degree as unclassified. This differs from the general CGPA classification table in the same supplied notes; verify the applicable Nursing Science regulations before relying on either statement.

Continuous assessment may include tests, written assignments and reports set by departments. The supplied handbook indicates continuous assessment contributes 20–30% of the total mark, with the final examination contributing the remainder. Semester examinations typically last one to three hours. The handbook summary says re-sit examinations are not permitted.

Academic standing

The handbook summary states a minimum CGPA of 1.0 for good standing, or 1.5 for students admitted from 2013 onward. It describes a warning (WN) for a first low-CGPA instance, first probation (P1st) after two consecutive semesters, and second probation (P2) after three consecutive semesters. Students required to withdraw are said to need admission into another faculty or department within four weeks of notice.

CGPA & classification notes

CGPA is calculated by multiplying each course's grade points by its units, summing the weighted scores and dividing by total units taken.

How to read the course tables

  • Course codes: the three-letter prefix identifies the teaching unit; the first digit in the number generally indicates the academic level.
  • C — Compulsory: mandatory departmental courses.
  • R — Required: general university-wide core courses.
  • E — Elective: a course selected to satisfy programme unit requirements.
  • Pre-requisite: a preliminary course; the supplied handbook summary states a minimum 35% score before registering for a dependent course.
  • LH / PH: lecture hours / practical hours. A dash means no hours were listed in the supplied extract; an asterisk is retained where the extract used one.

Course selection must follow the approved semester prospectus and advice from the academic adviser or Head of Department. The handbook summary says course changes and programme transfers must follow official procedures, with programme changes generally restricted to the first three weeks and 100/200 levels.

PROGRAMME REQUIREMENTS

Credit Units & Clinical Experience

Credit-unit system

Courses are taught and examined within a semester. The supplied handbook says standard courses normally carry 1–3 units; courses above 3 units require Senate approval, with a stated maximum of 4 units.

Clinical & practical learning

The curriculum includes practical sessions and clinical/community practicum courses, including entries labelled SIWES and nursing practicum. Practical hours are retained as transcribed from the supplied extracts.

Registration & withdrawal

Students should register within the university's specified window, pay required fees, retain receipts and obtain departmental/faculty endorsements. The supplied handbook summary says failure to register for two consecutive semesters may lead to forfeiture of studentship. Course withdrawal requires HOD authorisation through approved add/delete forms; an unapproved drop may result in failure.

Matriculation & online registration

Fresh students join the university through matriculation, sign the Matriculation Oath and receive a matriculation number for official records. The supplied handbook directs students to the AAUA student portal, to clear outstanding courses first, print registration forms for endorsement by the HOD and Dean, and return signed forms to the department. Add/delete forms are available through the faculty office during the approved period.
